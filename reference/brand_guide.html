Logo Color Typography Tokens Components Product UI Voice Code

Brand guide · derived from assetliving.com · 2026-11-21

A confident navy, a warm cream, and one italic word per headline.

This guide records the visual system of Asset Living's public website so a product prototype can feel native to it. Every token is taken from the site's stylesheet and markup unless it carries an inferred or proposed label.

Editorial serif

Freight Display at weight 400 for every heading. Large, light, never bold.

Navy and cream

Sections alternate between dark blue, deep blue, cream and white.

Square corners

Buttons, cards, images and inputs have a radius of zero. Shadows are near invisible.

Sparing accents

Orange, green and sky blue appear as small marks: an active tab, a quote glyph, an icon.

03 · Typography

A light serif over a geometric sans

Headings

Freight Display Pro

CSS name freight-display-pro. Loaded from Adobe Fonts (Typekit kit xdb8bdk), weights 400 to 700 with italics. The site uses weight 400 only. Licensed.

Substitute in this guide (inferred choice): Source Serif 4 from Google Fonts. The site also self-hosts Source Serif Pro for a few italic accents, so it is already in the family.

Body and UI

Gilroy

Self-hosted OTF files on the site's CDN, weights 100 to 900. Body is 400; nav and labels are 500; names on cards are 600. Commercial license. Do not hot-link the site's font files.

Substitute in this guide (inferred choice): Figtree from Google Fonts, a geometric sans with similar proportions and good tabular figures for data.

Script accent

Team Asset

Gustav, self-hosted OTF, one weight. Used only for the handwritten "Team Asset" wordmark at 2.5 to 3rem. Larken (Adobe Fonts) appears as an italic blog-category label.

Substitute (inferred choice): Caveat. Leave the script out of a product UI unless a marketing moment calls for it.

Conventions (extracted)

Italic emphasis word. One word or short phrase per headline is wrapped in a span set to italic. This is the site's signature typographic move. No color change, no weight change.

07 · Voice and tone

Proud, people-first, plain-spoken

Quoted from the site

  • “Driving success for our partners and the places people call home.” Homepage headline
  • “Proudly challenging the limits of what property management can accomplish.” Homepage
  • “Leadership isn’t reflected in your title — it’s a mentality that’s ingrained into who we are and how we serve.” Leadership page
  • “Be a part of something bigger.” Careers call to action
  • “Everyone deserves a place to call home.” Mission statement, About overview
  • “We’re here to support.” Contact page

Recurring words: partner, community, team, proven, trusted, coast-to-coast, nationwide, forward-thinking, tailored. The handwritten “Team Asset” is the internal rallying name.

Traits (inferred from the copy)

First person plural
“We” and “our” speak to “you” and “your”. Clients are “partners”, staff are “team members”, properties are “communities”.
Confident, backed by numbers
Claims arrive with a figure: 455K units, 12K employees, 800+ clients, 40+ years, 40 states.
Warm over corporate
“The places people call home”, “heartfelt gratitude”. Sentences are short and declarative.
Headlines in sentence or title case
Never all caps. One italic word carries the emphasis. Buttons are two or three words: “Learn More”, “Contact Us”, “Explore Career Opportunities”.

In product UI (proposed)

  • Page title: “Your communities, at a glance”
  • Empty state: “No open work orders. Your teams are caught up.”
  • Error: “We couldn’t load this report. Try again, or contact your Asset Living partner.”
  • Actions: name the thing. “Add community”, “Send owner report”. Not “Submit” or “OK”.
