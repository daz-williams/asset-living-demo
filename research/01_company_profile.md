**How to read this file**

- Every material fact carries a source link and the year of the figure.
- **[Inference]** marks my own reasoning. **[Unverified]** marks a claim I found in only one weak source or could not confirm. **[Conflict]** marks places where sources disagree.
- Most numbers are company self-reported (website, press releases). Independent figures are the NMHC Top 50 counts and trade-press reporting.

## 3. Ownership, capital structure and financials

**What is reasonably established**

- Family-controlled origins: Michael McGrath bought the business from the founder's estate; his son Ryan McGrath has been CEO and president since 2018 ([Multifamily Executive, Mar 2022](https://www.multifamilyexecutive.com/property-management/asset-living-accelerates-its-growth-goals_o)).
- Trilantic North America (private equity) invested in 2018 and lists the investment as "Realized", meaning it has exited ([Trilantic portfolio page](https://trilanticnorthamerica.com/portfolio/asset-living)). Trilantic was still publishing Asset Living news as a portfolio company in January 2021 ([Trilantic news, Jan 2021](https://www.trilanticnorthamerica.com/news/asset-living-acquires-texas-based-alpha-barnes-real-estate-leading-property-management-company-adds-one-of-the-largest-property-management-firms-for-affordable-housing)).
- June 1, 2026: Reuters reported (as relayed by trade press) that New Mountain Capital agreed to acquire Asset Living from Roark Capital for more than $2 billion, in partnership with CEO Ryan McGrath, who stays involved. Roark had been exploring a sale with William Blair. The deal reportedly includes "Asset Living's real estate portfolio and its proprietary technology suite" ([Bisnow, Jun 3, 2026](https://www.bisnow.com/national/news/multifamily/new-mountain-capital-set-to-acquire-asset-living-2b-134848); [CRE Daily, Jun 5, 2026](https://www.credaily.com/briefs/new-mountain-capital-acquires-asset-living-in-2b-deal/)).
- Asset Living told Bisnow the report "contains inaccuracies" and declined to elaborate. All parties declined to comment as of June 1, 2026 ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/national/news/multifamily/new-mountain-capital-set-to-acquire-asset-living-2b-134848), [CRE Daily](https://www.credaily.com/briefs/new-mountain-capital-acquires-asset-living-in-2b-deal/)).

**What is not established**

- **[Unverified] When and how Roark Capital became the owner.** I found no primary announcement. One commentary article says Roark acquired the company in 2021 ([Business News Today](https://business-news-today.com/can-new-mountains-2bn-asset-living-deal-turn-rental-housing-management-into-a-private-equity-prize/)); a wiki-style site says a sale happened in 2022 with William Blair advising. Neither is authoritative, and they disagree.
- **[Unverified] Whether the New Mountain deal has closed.** Reports in June 2026 described it as agreed and expected to close after customary approvals. I found no closing announcement from New Mountain, Roark or Asset Living. One low-quality aggregator claims it closed; I do not rely on it.
- **[Unverified] The exact stake held by McGrath/management** before or after the transaction.
- **[Conflict] Owned real estate.** In 2019 the CEO said: "We intentionally do not have an ownership interest in any properties to avoid any conflict of interest with clients" ([rebrand release](https://www.assetliving.com/blogs/asset-campus-housing-rebrands-as-asset-living)). The 2026 deal reports refer to an owned "real estate portfolio" being included. Either the position changed, or this is one of the "inaccuracies" the company alluded to. Unresolved.

### General industry mechanics — not sourced to Asset Living

I found no public information on Asset Living's actual fee rates or contract terms. The following is **general industry knowledge, stated as [Inference / unverified for this company]**:

- The base management fee is normally a percentage of collected revenue (commonly in the 2.5–4% range for institutional conventional multifamily, lower for very large assets, often higher or regulator-capped for affordable and higher for student and small assets), usually with a monthly minimum.
- On-site staff are employed by the manager but their payroll and benefits are reimbursed by the property owner. This matters: site-level labor savings accrue mainly to the owner's NOI, while corporate and shared-services savings accrue to the manager's margin.
- Ancillary revenue typically includes construction/capex management fees, lease-up and takeover fees, technology or marketing charge-backs, insurance program participation, procurement rebates, compliance fees (affordable), and incentive fees tied to NOI.
- Management agreements are usually one-year terms that renew automatically and can be terminated by the owner on about 30 days' notice without cause; contracts also end when the property is sold. Accounts are therefore lost through owner dissatisfaction (performance, reporting, staffing turnover), asset sales, and owners consolidating managers or taking management in-house; they are won at acquisition, at new development lease-up, and by displacing an underperforming incumbent.

These terms should be confirmed with the client before they are built into the prototype's assumptions.

**[Inference] What "customer churn" means here.** For a third-party manager the customer is the property owner, and churn is the loss of management contracts (by property or by client). Resident turnover is a second, separate churn metric that drives the owner's results. The prototype should be explicit about which one it targets. The flat 2024–2025 unit count (section 4) suggests gross contract losses roughly offset gross wins that year.

## 13. Implications for an AI-first operating layer

These are my inferences from the facts above, not company statements.

1. **The "layer above the PMS" thesis fits their reality.** They run Yardi, RealPage and Entrata side by side, describe their data architecture as "PMS-agnostic", and have absorbed at least eleven operators with their own systems and processes. A prototype should show it working across more than one PMS and normalizing data, because that is their daily problem.
2. **Position as extending what they have, not replacing it.** They already own a BI platform, owner portals, an EliseAI deployment for leasing and collections, and an AI roadmap owned by the Chief Transformation Officer. The "proprietary technology suite" was part of the sale value. A demo that ignores this will look naive; one that orchestrates across it (BI data in, EliseAI and PMS actions out) will land better.
3. **Aim at what EliseAI does not cover.** Leasing, delinquency reminders, maintenance intake and renewals conversations are taken. Open ground appears to be: owner/client retention and reporting, regional-manager workload, property accounting and AP, affordable compliance, property transitions and takeovers, budgeting variance commentary, staffing-gap management, and procurement.
4. **Owner (client) churn is the strategic metric.** Units were flat from the 2024 to the 2025 NMHC list before FPI. Contracts are typically short-notice. An "account health" module that predicts at-risk owner relationships from operating KPIs, reporting timeliness, staffing instability and owner communications would speak directly to the CEO, the Chief Sales Officer and the Client Services organization. Burrows, the new COO, came from the owner side (Nuveen) and will recognize the owner's view immediately.
5. **Manager-to-door efficiency maps to the regional manager and shared-services tiers.** Only about 35K units use centralized roles, under 10% of the portfolio. The headroom is large, and the company markets a "low regional manager-to-property ratio" as a service feature, so the pitch should be "same attention per property with wider spans", not "fewer managers".
6. **Affordable housing is the differentiating vertical.** It is their No. 1 ranking, roughly 137K units, the focus of current hiring, and it carries heavy compliance work (LIHTC, HUD recertifications, inspections). An affordable-specific workflow in the demo would be distinctive; most proptech demos assume conventional multifamily.
7. **Integration is a live pain point.** FPI (165K units) was absorbed barely a year ago; training and performance roles are still being hired in Sacramento. Tools that speed onboarding of acquired portfolios and newly won properties (data migration checks, lease file audits, SOP alignment) match the Property IT and Client Services remit and the due-diligence service line.
8. **The economics have two buyers.** Site payroll is generally reimbursed by owners, so on-site savings mostly improve the owner's NOI (a retention and sales argument), while corporate, regional and shared-services savings improve Asset Living's own margin (the PE owner's argument). The prototype's value case should show both lines separately. Revenue-per-door also grows through ancillary attach (insurance program, construction management, procurement, Poetic marketing), which an AI layer could prompt.
9. **Use their language.** "Platform", "operational excellence", "people-first", "automate the routine so our associates can focus on high-impact, human-first resident interactions", "partner", "everyone deserves a place to call home". Public messaging frames AI as capacity release and burnout prevention, not headcount reduction, although the FPI back-office cuts show cost removal is real. The demo should lead with capacity and service quality and let the efficiency numbers speak for themselves.
10. **Likely stakeholders.** Cory Hitte (AI and innovation roadmap, shared services, acquisition integration) is the natural executive sponsor; Marti Burrows (operations consistency); Codie Riggs (data and automation); Joe Goodwin (platform services, risk); David Walther (partnerships, sponsor of the EliseAI deal); JC Reeves and the Client Services EVPs for anything touching owner retention; Lance Amy for procurement use cases.
11. **Timing.** A reported $2B+ change of ownership in 2026, a first-reported COO, a CMO search, and a vendor-partnership build-out all point to an organization actively redesigning its operating model. New PE ownership usually brings a value-creation plan with explicit margin targets, which makes measurable efficiency metrics in the demo more important than feature breadth.
12. **Build-versus-buy risk.** The AI Architect and Forward Deployed Engineer postings suggest they are building internal capability. A prototype is more credible as an accelerator for that team (open, composable, PMS-agnostic) than as a closed product.
